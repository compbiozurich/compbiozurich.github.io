#### Geert van Geest (UB & SIB)

The life sciences domain thrives on shared data. From reference databases to
community-curated knowledgebases, the field’s progress rests on data that are open
and FAIR: findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable. This lecture starts
from that foundation, drawing on resources developed at the SIB Swiss Institute
of Bioinformatics, and explores why FAIR principles matter more than ever now that
AI models depend on large volumes of well-annotated, machine-actionable data. <!--more-->

This goes beyond data: the analyses built on them must also be reproducible and reusable.
We will discuss how reproducible workflows impact progression in science. At the
same time, AI is rapidly changing how bioinformaticians write code, explore data
and interpret results, bringing new opportunities but also new risks to rigour and
understanding. We close by reflecting on the skills a bioinformatician needs in
this ever-evolving landscape, and on how training can help researchers to perform
solid and innovative bioinformatics analyses.
